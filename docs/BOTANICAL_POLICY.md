# Botanical Evidence and Safety Policy

## Purpose

Digital Flora is an educational botanical catalogue. It must present uncertainty clearly and must not treat an image-model result as taxonomic proof.

## Identification policy

- Photographs are assessed by visible diagnostic characters, not by a model label alone.
- A species-level name requires sufficient distinguishable traits or independent expert/authoritative verification.
- Where a photograph cannot support species identification, the record remains `probable`, `genus_only` or `unidentified`.
- Similar taxa and missing diagnostic traits must be documented in `possible_lookalikes` and `uncertainty_note`.

## Claims policy

- Every claim about toxicity, edibility, medicinal use, contraindication, animal safety or invasive status requires a suitable source.
- Traditional use may be described only when labelled as traditional use; it is not evidence of efficacy or safety.
- Do not publish dosage, diagnosis, treatment advice or claims that a plant is safe to consume.
- Absence of a risk claim does not mean absence of risk. Use `insufficient evidence` when reliable evidence is unavailable.

## Required review

Editorial review is mandatory before publication. The editor must verify taxonomy, record uncertainty, check image association, and review safety-related text and sources. AI may create or enrich drafts but cannot approve publication.

## Source quality

Prefer floras, peer-reviewed literature, national/international botanical institutions, biodiversity databases with curation, poison-control/veterinary authorities and regulatory sources. Store enough bibliographic metadata to audit the specific claim.

## Corrections

If an identification or safety claim is disputed, unpublish or downgrade the affected record pending review; preserve the original content and revision history.
